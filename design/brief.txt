
PERSONAL BRAND WEBSITEDESIGN + CONTENT BRIEF
For Claude — design direction, UX, visual language and implementation
1. THE BIG IDEA
Create a premium personal-brand website that feels like entering an intelligent studio, library or private club—not a conventional consultancy website. The brand helps independent/lifestyle builders think clearly before they build.
Core positioning: Strategic counsel for people who want to build something of their own—but want to think it through before they build it.
Brand promise: THINK BEFORE YOU BUILD.
2. BRAND FEEL
Intelligent, editorial, contemporary and quietly premium.
Warm and human rather than corporate.
Confident without being loud.
Approachable, but not casual or gimmicky.
A little irreverent: dry humour appears occasionally between serious sections.
Modern editorial publication + private members' club + thoughtful strategist's studio.
3. VOICE &amp; COPY RULES
Use short paragraphs, strong headlines, generous whitespace and occasional one-line asides.
Humour should be dry, intelligent and sparse—not comedy.
Avoid generic consultant language: unlock, empower, transform, disrupt, scale your potential, etc.
Avoid aggressive sales language.
Prefer clarity over jargon.
The writing should sometimes feel like an intelligent friend quietly making an observation.
Serious proposition first; humour is the wink.
Example: “A beautifully designed plan that never leaves the notebook is, technically speaking, stationery.”
4. PRIMARY NAVIGATION

Primary CTA: START THINKING / LET'S THINK. Secondary CTA: WORK WITH ME.

6. INNER PAGE: THINK
Headline: Before the answer, there is usually a better question.
Problems often arrive looking simple: “I need a new business.” “I don't know what to do next.” “I have an idea.” The THINK space unpacks what is underneath.
Content pillars: THE SITUATION · THE PROBLEM · THE INTENTION · THE OPTIONS · THE CONSEQUENCES
CTA: EXPLORE THE THINKING LIBRARY
7. INNER PAGE: DECIDE
Headline: Clarity is useful. Decisions are better.
Cover starting, changing, stopping and choosing between options. Emphasise that a decision does not have to be perfect; it has to be good enough to move forward with.
CTA: EXPLORE DECISION TOOLS
8. INNER PAGE: BUILD
Headline: Ideas are lovely. Useful ideas are better.
Building means knowing what needs to be built first. Avoid the trap of making everything impressive before the actual offer works.
Principle: START WITH WHAT MATTERS → MAKE IT WORK → MAKE IT BETTER.
9. LIBRARY / PRODUCTS
Position the library as a curated collection rather than a generic shop.
I'M STUCK — a practical reset for when something isn't working but you cannot quite explain what.
SITUATION DECONSTRUCTION — a framework for taking a messy situation apart and seeing what is actually going on.
Future tools: decision-making, business ideas, offers, positioning, priorities and planning.
10. WORK WITH ME
Headline: Some problems are better solved together.
Offer: Strategic counsel for people building something of their own.
Process: 01 BRING THE SITUATION → 02 DISSECT IT → 03 THINK → 04 DECIDE → 05 MOVE
Tone line: “This isn't coaching theatre. There will be no dramatic breakthrough music. Just good questions, honest thinking and useful decisions.”
11. ABOUT PAGE
Do not make this a standard biography. Make it editorial and belief-led.
You don't need to scale everything.
Not every idea deserves a business.
Being busy is not a strategy.
A smaller business can be a very successful business.
Stopping can be progress.
Sometimes the smartest move is to do less—properly.
12. VISUAL DIRECTION
Use the supplied professional portrait direction as the primary photography style.
Professional outfits: tailored trousers, crisp shirts, structured blazers, elegant business-casual looks.
Avoid stereotypical corporate stock photography.
Photography should feel natural, editorial and confident—not posed or overly glamorous.
Mix polished portraits with carefully selected lifestyle images to show range.
Do not use too many visually similar white-shirt portraits together.
Use generous negative space so typography can breathe around photography.
Consider large portrait crops, editorial image blocks and occasional full-bleed photography.
13. UI / UX DIRECTION
Minimal interface; content is the hero.
Strong typographic hierarchy and large editorial headlines.
Generous whitespace and calm pacing.
Subtle motion only: slow image reveals, understated hover states, smooth transitions.
Avoid excessive cards, gradients, animated counters, floating widgets and generic SaaS patterns.
Navigation should remain extremely clear.
Product/library pages should feel curated and tactile.
Use small editorial asides or pull quotes as visual breaks.
14. COLOUR / TYPOGRAPHY
Start with a restrained, sophisticated palette: warm white/off-white, near-black, soft neutrals and one restrained accent. Combine a strong editorial display face with a highly readable modern sans-serif. The overall effect should feel premium, intelligent and timeless.
15. WHAT TO AVOID
Generic consulting website aesthetic.
Corporate blue, generic gradients or stock-photo business imagery.
Overuse of motivational language.
Too many CTAs competing on one screen.
Excessive humour.
Trying to look like a luxury fashion brand at the expense of clarity.
A website that feels like a template marketplace.
Making the founder look inaccessible.
16. DESIGN REFERENCE PRINCIPLE
The Rochambeau Club is a reference for editorial confidence, wit, personality and the feeling of a world with its own point of view. Do NOT copy its visual identity, wording or structure. Use the principle: polished presentation + strong personality + unexpected dry humour.
17. THE EXPERIENCE WE WANT
A visitor should feel:
“This person is smart.”
“This feels different from a normal consultant website.”
“I trust the thinking.”
“I could actually use these tools.”
“I like the personality.”
“I understand what I can do next.”
